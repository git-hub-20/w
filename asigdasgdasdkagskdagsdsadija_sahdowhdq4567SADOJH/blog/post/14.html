Coping Ourselves to Death

Sometimes I wonder about happiness and fulfillment as I see many people with tendencies to—presumably subconsciously—fabricate and manufacture rationalized stances, values, and character traits, which seem to be made just to fit the thin hole in reality that allows them to maintain their illusion of happiness.

I see it when it comes to big life decisions, careers, health, lifestyle, relationships, or politics. Just one big pile of self-preserving rationalizing and self-illusioning mechanisms. All of this is one big cope that modern society is built upon, with most people not realizing they are one step in front of the biggest cognitive dissonance of their lives.

Yet it trickles down. Even if not heard and ignored, something inside us is screaming, trying to relieve us from this prison, while the rest of the self—presumably protecting integrity and safety in the current stressful environment—allows only a fraction of restful moments. But minute after minute, it is accumulating, leading to bursts of compensation here and there. I think that is one of the reasons why today is such a messy place.

On the other hand, introspection, getting to know yourself better, and the deconstruction of the present and its myth has always been a tough process, yet today it is almost impossible with persistent pressure, stimuli, endless possibilities, and information overload. Has this world become a place where the most effective strategy is to cope ourselves to death?
